# UX review — 3 August 2026

A point-in-time review of **both** sibling sites, kept together because most items
compare them: [Earth × Time](https://kejjeh.github.io/earth-x-time/) and
[Human × Time](https://kejjeh.github.io/human-x-time/). The identical file lives in
both repos so neither depends on the other.

**This is a frozen snapshot, not a live backlog.** Line numbers and measurements are
as of this date and will rot. Nothing here has been implemented.

## How it was produced

Six independent lenses — first-thirty-seconds, discovery, detail-panel depth, touch,
appeal, accessibility — each read the source and proposed features. Every proposal
then went to an adversary instructed to kill it, with four specific grounds: it
already exists, it contradicts a thesis the README argues for, it cannot run under
the strict CSP / no-network / single-concatenated-script constraints, or its
mechanism names something that does not exist. 46 proposals in, 43 through the
adversary, 12 items after the synthesiser cut for redundancy and scope.

The touch and accessibility lenses drove the built pages in headless Chromium at
390×844 with touch emulation rather than reasoning from the CSS; where a number
appears in those sections it was measured.

# Ranked recommendation: Earth × Time and Human × Time

Twelve items. Everything else is cut at the bottom with a reason. Effort labels are relative to this codebase: **S** = under an afternoon, **M** = a day or two.

The ranking is dominated by one observation the individual proposals each saw a corner of: **on a phone, both sites are close to non-functional**, and the failures are all cheap. Four of the top six are that. Two are comprehension failures on desktop that cost the sites their whole argument.

---

## The ranked list

### 1. Phone triage — three small fixes that decide whether the phone build works at all `both · S`

Merged from the mobile halves of the clipped-control-row, dead-tap, and off-screen-fly-to proposals. Individually none is a story; together they are the reason a phone visitor leaves in four seconds.

- **The stage control strip is clipped off the left edge.** `.stage-tr { position:absolute; top:10px; right:12px; display:flex }` (`Earth x Time\src\00_head.html:336`) is shrink-to-fit anchored right, inside `.stage { overflow:hidden }`. At 390px the row spans −64 to 294, so **Earth's "Guided path" button is entirely off-screen** — and `#btn-tour` is the *only* entry point to the tour: `S.tour` is not in `encodeHash()` and there is no keyboard binding. The site's own answer to "what am I looking at" is unreachable on every phone. Fix inside the `@media (max-width:860px)` block: `left:12px; right:12px; overflow-x:auto; justify-content:flex-start` plus `white-space:nowrap; flex:none` on `.iconbtn`. Use `flex-start`, not `flex-end` — content overflowing the *start* edge of a flex scroll container is unreachable in Chromium and WebKit. Also push `.stage-tl` down (`top:52px`), because right now the buttons sit on top of "As understood in 2026".
- **A tap on a marker produces nothing you can see.** `setSelection` is the single funnel (`Earth x Time\src\80_boot.js:23`, `Human Time\src\80_boot.js:19`), and `.instrument` — which holds the entire detail panel — is at grid row 3 under a 46vh stage. Measured: `detailTop` 1151px on Earth, 993px on Human, on an 855px viewport. The tooltip never fires on touch (it lives after `if (gDrag) { … return; }`). One line: when `matchMedia('(pointer:coarse)')` matches *and* the selection came from the canvas, `elDetail.scrollIntoView({block:'start'})`. It needs a source argument, not a heuristic — `elDetail.scrollTop = 0` is a no-op on mobile (`.detail { overflow: visible }`) and `setSelection(null)` from Escape must not scroll.
- **Choosing a search result flies the globe while the globe is off-screen.** Reproduced: Earth lands at `scrollY:108, stageTop:-108`. Scroll `.stage` into view before `flyTo` / `flyToEvent`. No-op on desktop.

Also fold in here: the cluster cell `Math.max(15, GR * 0.055)` (`Human Time\src\45_markers.js:127`) evaluates to the floor 15 on a phone where `GR` is 128 versus 18 on desktop — the cell essentially does not move while the globe halves. Make the floor coarse-pointer-aware. Do **not** also grow the hit radii; 114 of 128 tap points already fall inside more than one hit circle, and a bigger radius grows the ambiguity.

### 2. Trying to scroll the page destroys the view `both · M`

Reproduced with synthesised touch swipes at 390×844: a 250px upward swipe on the globe takes `S.rot.phi` from 12 to −89 (clamped at the pole) with `scrollY` still 0; a 200px swipe on the rail moves Earth's `S.kt` from 2026 to 1943. The top ~380px of the first screen refuses to scroll and silently rewrites state instead. Both surfaces are `touch-action: none` (`Earth …\00_head.html:370`, `Human …\00_head.html:260`).

Mechanism: `touch-action: pan-y` under coarse pointers on `#globe`, `#krailcv`/`#railcv`, with a horizontal-dominant one-pointer drag still rotating. The pinch path is unaffected, and the existing `pointercancel` bookkeeping (`Earth …\80_boot.js:174-191`) already does the right thing when the browser steals the gesture. **Be honest about the trade:** `railSet`/`kSet` read only `e.clientY`, so under `pan-y` the vertical drag-scrub is *gone* on touch and tap-to-set becomes the only way to move the value. That is still the right call — an unrequested 80-year jump is worse than losing a scrub — but decide it deliberately.

### 3. Light-mode stage overlays fail contrast for every sighted visitor `both · S`

Human's `.stage` carries a dark radial gradient declared once (`Human Time\src\00_head.html:118-119`) and never overridden per theme, while `.sub` (`#hd-sub`) resolves to `--chalk-dim:#5D5449` in light mode: **2.70:1 at 12px**. Earth's `#epistemic` is 3.25:1, `.lbl`/`.presets`/`.empty` 2.98:1. These are exactly the small explanatory strings that tell a visitor what the axes mean. Fix: a stage-overlay token pair that does not flip with the theme, applied in the four token blocks (`Earth …\00_head.html:14-106`, `Human …\00_head.html:16-87`). Mandatory on Human, where the gradient is inescapable; on Earth it is escapable via the Chart basemap, so it is merely correct.

Drop the claim that 5.68:1 fails AA — it passes; small size forfeits the 3:1 large-text exemption, it does not raise the threshold.

### 4. `goTo()` — a click inside the panel must land somewhere you can see `both · S`

Choosing a search result is careful: `chooseResult` (`Earth x Time\src\75_search.js:96-120`) advances knowledge-time, clears focus, re-enables the subject, widens the window and flies. Clicking a link *inside* the panel is `setSelection(b.dataset.goto); elDetail.scrollTop = 0;` (`80_boot.js:354-358`) — forty lines of logic bypassed, under a comment forty lines away stating the rule the panel violates.

Two real consequences, both first-interaction: `items[id]` is written with no `inWindow`/`subjectOn` gate, so a hop renders a full panel for a referent with no mark anywhere; and if `resolve()` returns null at the current `S.kt`, `items[id]` is undefined and the panel **falls through to "Nothing selected" while `S.selection` is set** — it goes blank on a click.

One helper both sites share. Two corrections to the obvious port: gate the window-widen on "target is out of window" (Earth's `chooseResult` sets `[0, res.oldest*1.7]` unconditionally and would blow away a hand-panned window — Human's `chooseEvent` already gates it correctly at `75_search.js:91-94`); and drop "below the zoom band" from the diagnosis, because `30_model.js:147` already carries `|| A.selection === id`.

### 5. Stop rebuilding the whole panel on every state change `both · M`

`renderDetail()` reassigns `elDetail.innerHTML` unconditionally, from the `needPanel` block, and `needPanel` is set by `markAll()` inside `changed()`, which `setKt`/`setWindow`/`setSelection`/`setCoverage` all call. So every pointermove of a rail drag destroys and rebuilds the panel. Three consequences:

- **It breaks the site's own headline interaction.** `README.md:25` sells "Scrub 1950 → 2025 with *Peopling of the Americas* selected" — precisely the gesture that throws the reader back to the top of the claim list on every year.
- **The causal graph is keyboard-unwalkable.** The delegated handler fires on a button whose subtree is then replaced, so `activeElement` drops to `<body>`.
- **`#detail` is the `aria-live="polite"` region.** Replay sweeps 1650→2026 in 16s ≈ 23.5 `setKt` calls/second ≈ 72 full-panel re-announcements in three seconds. `#tip` is also `role="status"` and rewritten on every hover pointermove — stripping `aria-live` from `#detail` alone just moves the flood; make `#tip` `aria-hidden`.

Mechanism: a content signature (`S.selection`, `S.kt`, `S.resolver`, `F.allEdges.length` are all in scope), scroll/focus restore, volatile numbers into their own nodes updated by `textContent`, and `aria-live` moved onto a one-line `.sr` status node — the `.sr` utility is already defined and unused at `Earth …\00_head.html:140`. **Say out loud that this reverses a documented decision** (`Earth x Time\src\10_body.html:44-45` states panel-as-live-region as deliberate).

Fold in the same-class bug on Human: `70_panel.js:41-44` allocates and comparator-sorts a 38,242-element array per pointer event during a chron pan. That is the exact cost the README brags about having removed; it survived the pass. Replace with the 34-slot bounded insertion at `45_markers.js:268-280`.

### 6. Earth: turn the knowledge rail into a table of contents for the argument `earth · M`

The highest-value item on the list. Earth's second axis is the whole product, and at rest it is seven bare fifty-year numbers beside an unlabelled grey shape. A visitor who does drag it is as likely to land on 1740, where nothing moves, as on 1980, where the page changes. Four merged pieces, all in `Earth x Time\src\60_krail.js` plus a hover-Y in `80_boot.js`:

- **Named landmarks.** `30_model.js:261-270` hard-codes 1700/1800/1862/1907/1956/1980/1991/2000/2015 as caption thresholds and the README's "Try these" leads with 1975/1991/1880. The product knows where its interesting years are; the control that reaches them does not say.
- **Hover readout.** Neither rail has a bare `pointermove` — both are pointerdown / move-guarded-by-drag / up / cancel. "1980 · 6 claims first made, 2 links wired" turns a grey pip into an invitation. Derive "links" from `GRAPH.edges` via `R.claims[e.claim_id].knowledge_time`, not from `status_timeline` entries — a status transition is a claim hardening, not an edge appearing.
- **Next / previous change year.** 115 distinct years carry a change, min 1669, largest gap 119 years. Two narrow buttons in one row of `.krail-foot` (it is a 104px column, 84px at the 1080px breakpoint, with `.iconbtn { width:100% }`).
- **Supersession pips.** 402 real transitions over 76 years, currently rendered nowhere. `KT_PIPS` shows arrivals only.

Two collisions to solve rather than ignore: `KT_PIPS` bars already occupy the left channel (`spine` at `KW*0.62`, bars up to `4 + 0.3*KW` wide leftward, leaving ~33px clear at 104px and ~21px at 84px), and `spine + 3` is already the selected-claim pip column with tick labels at `spine + 8`. One of the two has to move, or the rail widens.

Cheap Human counterpart, same idea, `Human Time\src\60_rail.js`: a hover readout on the survivor curve — "at 12+ editions · 9,204 of 38,242 in the corpus". Say *in the corpus*, since `SURVIVORS` is built from all of `EV`, not the window; "events" unqualified will contradict `#hd-n` and read as a bug.

Precompute the derivations once at load, not per bucket — 75 buckets × 52 edges × `statusAt` is fine; 57 `resolve()` calls per bucket is ~150 full graph queries and is not.

### 7. Earth: name the other 33 subjects, and say why each is not on screen `earth · M`

`queryFacts` stores `{inWindow, subjectOn, zoomOK, inFocus, rolledUp}` per referent at `30_model.js:151-158` and collapses all five to one boolean at `:174`, throwing the reasons away. `epistemicCaption` prints "24 of 57 subjects visible" and stops. Nothing anywhere enumerates the other 33; the empty panel lists only the five widest disputes *among the already-visible*, and search requires you to know the name first. Verified: all 57 resolve at kt=2026, exactly 24 pass the zoom gate at the default window.

Render the roster with a per-item reason and a click that reveals it. The reveal works because of the escape hatch at `30_model.js:147` — `|| A.selection === id` — not because of window widening; a 66 Ma referent with `zoomMin` 7 at a `res.oldest * 1.7` window gives z≈1.9 and stays hidden. State it as the selection override or a reader will build the wrong thing. **Do not render into `#subjects`** — `tools\smoke_test.py:192-194` asserts exactly 6 children.

### 8. Human: make the category a real axis `human · M`

62 real Wikidata classes ship in `src\events.json` as `{key,label,theme}`, are collapsed to 6 themes by `CAT_THEME`, surfaced per event as `e.c`, and rendered as an inert `<span class="tag">` at `70_panel.js:48-49`. `CAT_LABEL` is read at exactly one place in the codebase. "Show me the other cathedrals" is unaskable, though there are 1,205 of them; "building" is 21 categories and 16,175 events behind one chip.

Make the tag a control: click pins the category, a dismissible chip appears beside the theme chips, `queryEvents` gains one `cat` axis (consistent with `30_query.js`'s own header, "one query with different axes pinned" — this is not a second filtering path). Three traps: validate the hash value with `CATS.includes(ct)`, **not** `hasOwnProperty` — `CATS` is an Array, so that accepts `"length"` and every index; `catCounts[e.c]++` must go after the theme test and before the category test or every deselected category reports zero; and 62 rows above `.detail { flex:1 1 auto }` will squeeze the panel to nothing, so it needs a bounded scroller or per-theme disclosure. Decide what a pinned category does when its parent theme is off.

Free crumb while in there: put `CAT_LABEL[e.c]` in the tooltip, which currently shows only name, year and language count.

### 9. Human: a guided path that moves the axes rather than flying to points `human · M`

`grep -c tour` returns 0 across Human's head, body and boot. Earth's tour flies between named points, which is exactly wrong here — no single one of 38,242 events is the point. Five steps that each move a *control*: name the axes; drop the floor to 1 and watch 2,183 become 38,242; set the lens to "Missing from en.wikipedia" and watch 7,387 survive; open the window and point at the histogram thickening toward the present; rotate to Central Asia at a fixed floor. That script is already written — it is the README's own "Try these" table.

Pitch it as *demonstration*, not as fixing an unstated thesis: the thesis is on the default screen in four places already. Copy `TOUR`/`tourGo`/`flyTo` from `Earth x Time\src\80_boot.js:406-453`, the markup from `10_body.html:122-129`, the CSS from `00_head.html:417-428`. Two mechanism notes: `flyToEvent` takes an EV index and unconditionally calls `setSelection`, so lift out the three-line longitude unwrap for a rotation-only step; and setting `S.lens` then calling `changed()` will not move the `<select>` — `renderLens` returns early forever once `dataset.built` is set, so call `syncControls()`.

### 10. Close the keyboard loop `both · S`

Four small, unrelated-looking defects that are one story: the sites advertise keyboard operability they do not have.

- **Focus on the globe is invisible.** `:focus-visible { outline-offset: 2px }` paints 2–4px *outside* the canvas border box; `#globe` is `width:100%; height:100%` of `.stage`, which is `overflow:hidden`. Every pixel of the ring is clipped, on a `role="application" tabindex="0"` element whose arrow keys hijack page scroll. One CSS line per site (inset the outline, or a box-shadow ring). `#chroncv` and `#krailcv`/`#railcv` are fine — only the globe is broken.
- **Human's time axis declares `role="slider"` and has no `keydown`.** Five listeners on `ccv`, none of them keyboard. Adapt rather than port Earth's handler — Human has no `S.cursor`, so its arrows must pan. Note the stronger case nobody made: on *both* sites no keyboard user can move the window's near edge off zero, because `+`/`-` anchor on `S.win.t0` and every preset is `data-t0="0"`.
- **`aria-valuemax="300"` against a real `MAX_SL` of 289**, on a slider whose scale is `Math.log`. Write `MAX_SL` in `boot()` and add `aria-valuetext` using the existing `fmtYear`/`fmtYbpLabel` formatters.
- **Earth's Compare-eras dialog is `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"` with no focus management and no `inert`** — it declares the document hidden from AT while leaving focus on `#btn-diff`, inside the part it just hid, with twelve controls still tabbable behind it.

Order matters: **do item 5 first.** Moving focus into `#detail` before `aria-live` comes off it makes things worse, and the tour's step prose would queue behind a 10KB panel re-read.

### 11. Human: keep the promise the tooltip already makes `human · M`

The tooltip says `+7 more here`; the click resolves to one member and discards the rest. `45_markers.js:65` keeps only the best-covered member per cell and `:70-76` writes six scalars per bin, throwing membership away. The UI makes a promise the click cannot keep, and on touch the tooltip never appears at all, so a phone visitor does not even learn the mark is plural. Measured at 390×844: 128 groups covering 1,866 events, 87 with more than one member, top counts 216/197/141.

Recovery is one linear pass over `PI/PX/PY` (module-level scratch, survives the frame), off the frame path — the same shape as `countMatches`. Three things the proposal missed: `hitTest` stores `GI[k]` and `GN[k]` but never `k`, so add a parallel `HK`; `clusterInto` iterates `BINS.values()` and never sees the key; and take the member snapshot *inside* `endGlobeDrag` before `setSelection` repaints over the scratch. Also: bin membership is screen-space, and rotation sets only `needGlobe`, never `needPanel` — so either invalidate on rotate or the list silently describes a projection from several drags ago.

### 12. Follow the source, and make the link look like something `both · S`

Three small things that all concern what leaves the page.

- **Earth has zero outbound anchors in its entire source tree**, on a site whose panel header says nothing may show a date without showing who said so. 52 claims carry a `doi` key, 43 non-null; the field is decoded into `R.claims` and read by nothing. Render `asserted_by` as a DOI link where one exists and an honest "no DOI recorded" plus a search link where it does not. That weaker half is the stronger half: the README's own Known gaps says only the 37 causal-graph claims went through the adversarial citation pass and the earlier 238 are "plausible rather than checked" — a search link on an unchecked attribution is the site auditing itself. Drop the `status_timeline` sub-proposal; those entries carry only `{knowledge_time, status, source}`, no DOI.
- **Human's 32 language chips are inert spans.** All codes are plain and hyphen-free, so `Special:GoToLinkedPage` needs no mangling. A live `fr` chip beside a dead `sw` chip is the coverage argument made tactile. Do not pitch it as unlocking something unreachable — the Wikidata item's sidebar is two hops away today.
- **Neither built page has `og:`, `twitter:`, `description` or a favicon**, and both `preview.png` files are JPEG bytes under a `.png` name, referenced nowhere. GitHub Pages sets Content-Type from the extension, so a scraper gets JPEG bytes labelled `image/png` and several validators reject it outright — re-encode or rename before writing the tag. Also note `build.py` strips self-closed `<meta …/>` on its own line from `artifact.html`, and `<link rel="icon">` is not a `<meta` and would leak.

## The single highest-value thing, and the best ratio

**Highest value: #6, the Earth knowledge rail.** Everything else on this list changes whether a visitor can *operate* the site. That one changes whether they understand what it *is*. Earth × Time exists to show that a causal graph rewires as knowledge changes, and at the default view — `kt: KT_MAX`, nothing greyed, nothing missing, the "unknown future" fill an 18-pixel sliver — the second axis has literally nothing to show. Landmarks plus a hover readout turn a blank numeric scale into a visible table of contents for the argument.

**Best effort-to-payoff: the clipped stage strip in #1.** Roughly six lines of CSS inside an existing media query, no JS, no build risk. It takes Earth's six-step guided path — the site's own answer to "what am I looking at", and the only entry point to it — from *unreachable on every phone* to reachable.

**If there is only one afternoon:** do all of #1 and #3. They are the cheapest items on the list and they remove hard blockers rather than adding anything.

## What I cut, and why

- **Auto-playing an intro sweep of the second axis.** Earth already has Replay on a button, so the gain is only that it is unprompted; the Human half's premise is false (the coverage floor is named, counted and attributed on the stage before you touch anything); and a sweep from `KT_MIN` opens on an empty globe — **zero** referents have any asserted claim at 1650, and 1 at 1700. Item 6 solves the same comprehension problem without autoplay or a smoke-test rewrite.
- **A "Copy view" button.** The motivating case — the artifact embedding where `replaceState` throws — is exactly where `clipboard.writeText` needs a permission policy the host almost certainly did not grant, and `execCommand('copy')` from a sandboxed frame is commonly blocked too. The honest terminal fallback is a readonly pre-selected input, which is what the address bar already is on GitHub Pages. It also requires a hardcoded canonical origin string, a new kind of thing for these files to carry.
- **A note field in the share hash.** Depends on the cut button, would be the first free-text hash field (`encodeHash` encodes nothing and joins on `&`), and nobody types prose into a URL.
- **Present / kiosk mode.** Serves the author demoing the site more than the visitor. F11 plus the existing Replay reaches most of it, and hiding `.instrument` hides the only place a selection renders.
- **Per-theme coverage curves on Human's rail.** 38,048 of 38,242 events are in the default window, so the second curve is visually identical until someone filters hard, and the aggregate is already stated twice on the stage. The one worthwhile crumb is a small bug fix worth doing anyway: the coverage cut sits *above* `themeCounts[e.theme]++`, so the chips report post-cut counts (Disaster shows 58 against 2,950 in window). Move it, and rewrite `#hd-sub`/`#rd-drop` deliberately rather than letting `belowCoverage` silently change meaning.
- **A tablet-portrait tier.** Nothing is unreachable, only wasteful — and the proposed two-column grid has no bounded height to scroll against, because the ≤860 block sets `.app { height:auto }`.
- **A landscape-phone tier.** Real (844×390 gets a 179px globe) but narrow, and it needs a whole new breakpoint. A pixel `min-height` on the stage row is a one-line partial if it bothers you. Drop the "rail heading clipped mid-word" claim — it wraps, it does not clip.
- **A keyboard mark-walker.** Search already reaches any mark by keyboard on both sites and opens whatever filter hides it. On Human it would step through ~2,183 marks at the default view.
- **A `?` keyboard help sheet.** Six keyboard surfaces of prose to maintain for a small audience. Keep only the focus handoff after search (folded into #10) — `.focus()` appears exactly once per site, so `#detail`'s `tabindex="-1"` is declared and never used and focus lands on `<body>` after choosing a result.
- **Bigger hit radii and a scaled label budget.** The label half is disproved: `45_markers.js:296-301` already rejects intersecting boxes, and 24 labels actually draw at 390×844 versus 32 at desktop. `LMAX=34` is a candidate cap, not a draw count. The radius half is counterproductive on Human, where 114 of 128 taps already hit multiple targets. Only the cluster-cell floor survives, folded into #1.
- **Shape-coding markers by category.** `markerRadius` returns 2.4–8.4px for singles. Technically free, perceptually useless.
- **Click a stratigraphic band or century strip to frame it.** Earth's ribbon already responds — a stationary press anywhere in the chron places a cursor and `cursorContext` names every band under it. The genuinely new capability (a window with `t0 > 0`) collides with cursor placement in the same 82 pixels and with the `CHIT` hit test at y=65–70.
- **Rotation-dependent figures on Human's stage.** Serves the README's Europe/Central Asia demo, but the number would change continuously through a drag, which reads as noise; and publishing it correctly means writing from `drawEvents` with a string-compare guard, because rotation never sets `needPanel`.
- **"Nearby by great-circle" in Human's panel.** Needs a cap (478 events within 25km at the densest points, 90th percentile 135), and the panel already carries three lists. Keep "also at this mark" (#11); drop this.
- **Earth's BFS downstream chain.** The best thing on the cut list, and genuinely new — `edgesOut`/`edgesIn` are constructed and read by nothing. Cut on scope honesty: only 18 of 57 referents have a chain two hops or deeper, so it is a rich payoff on a third of the graph and a single row on the rest. **Take the cheap half now:** hovering an edge link brightens that arc, since the loop at `40_globe.js:569-595` already has `ed.edge.id` in hand. Note the affordance dead-ends for neighbours with no drawn arc (`allEdges` requires one endpoint in window, `liveEdges` requires both visible), and that `mouseenter` does not bubble.
- **"N referents move under Newest".** The cheapest thing on the cut list — maybe 30 minutes — and a nice way to make an inert segmented control look consequential. Cut only because it needs its own element (`#resnote` is `textContent`-overwritten from two places and neither fires on a knowledge-time change). Worth noting regardless: the README says "Ten referents move" and the correct number at kt=2026 is **28**. That is a documentation bug worth fixing today.

## What is already good, and should not be touched

Say no to any future proposal that requires relaxing these.

**The one-query architecture.** `queryFacts(S)` / `queryEvents(S)` is the single path every view reads, and both codebases honour it without exception. Several of the items above want a new axis; every one of them must land *inside* that query, as the category axis in #8 does. The moment there is a second filtering path, the sites stop being trustworthy about what they are showing.

**`resolve()` never returning a bare number.** It always returns the winner, the rivals and the provenance. So do the three rules that started as bugs and are documented as such: only rival datings set the band; superseded claims stay visible but stop stretching it; an edge keeps its far endpoint on screen. Each of those looks like a candidate for simplification and each is load-bearing. `disputed` being knowledge-time-dependent is a feature.

**Human's refusal of a knowledge-time axis.** It is argued with measurements — 16% of Wikidata date statements resolve to a citation, and Alvarez 1980's citation count *dips* across the very confirmation that settled the argument. That is the most intellectually honest paragraph across both projects. Nothing in this list asks for it back, and nothing should.

**The constraints: no framework, no WebGL, no network, strict CSP, and `smoke_test.py` as a build gate.** Also the columnar corpus that refuses to ship a payload that does not round-trip. These are why the sites are 12ms frames and one file. Item 5 and item 11 in particular must not reintroduce per-frame allocation — the parallel typed arrays, the 34-slot bounded insertion, and the batching by depth slab *then* colour are all there for reasons the README records.
